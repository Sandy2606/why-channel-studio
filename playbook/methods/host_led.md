# Methods: host-led editorial (tested treatment)

Method notes, not rules. The rule that lists this treatment is DIR-09; scripts plan it from the start (→ WF-01 step 3). Status: **tested and optional** (pilot approved Nov 13, 2026). Never forced into a video: the story decides.

## Goal (user, Nov 13)
Make the audience curious and keep them watching, without losing the channel's essence. Everything about the treatment may change if it serves that goal. That includes the host's movement: new poses or moves are welcome when they make him more human. His approved appearance and likeness stay as they are.

## What was tested (pilot: Delhi Rain Solapur beat, EN 18.7 s / TE 20.87 s, 9:16)
- Record: `videos/2026-11-13_test_host_led_editorial/`. The proposal, the preview report and the scores are there. The videos are not, because they show the likeness.
- **Host story turn.** One waist-up framing, at native size, over the soft-focus studio plate.
  - Mouth movement from Rhubarb cues; blinks; a brow raise on the question.
  - Stillness plus an amber strike-through on the answer.
  - A point toward the board on the cue line, an eye glance, and a small nod.
- **"He points, we cut."** The evidence lives on his board (frame right). When he points, cut to the proof.
- **Original evidence card** (Python 3D Data Chart):
  - both bars rise together as the card lands, so the comparison is complete at once;
  - the difference (a dashed baseline and an amber bracket, e.g. +18%) is drawn on the spoken number;
  - the baseline is labelled (e.g. RAINFALL INDEX · UNSEEDED = 100), with conditions on two lines;
  - the citation sits under the captions, the DOI as secondary text;
  - one restrained push-in across the card.
- **WHY? question reveal.** The amber dot lands, the brand "?" draws up from it, the WHY wordmark locks, and a short kicker (e.g. DELHI vs SOLAPUR) appears. A slow halftone drift keeps the hold alive. Used once per Short, at the central question.

## Writing for the host (at script stage)
- Give him the turns: the hook question, the twist, sometimes the tease. Typically 2–4 appearances in a Short, each under about 8 s, roughly a third of the runtime at most. Explanations stay on visuals.
- On-camera lines are short and direct. The narrator carries the explaining.
- Write the moves the art can do today:
  - talk, blink, raise the brows, glance at the board, nod;
  - point to the board (frame right), an open palm, a raised "tell me" palm.
- If a story needs a move the art doesn't have yet (a head shake, a smile, a turn, a walk-in, a lean), name it in the script. Then:
  - the art comes as free Gemini poses from the approved likeness;
  - the user checks the likeness;
  - any paid generation is priced and asked first (→ CR-01).

## Assets and privacy
- **The likeness assets are PRIVATE and never committed to this repository (it is public):** H_ref05, the reference artwork, the mouth and blink frames, the body poses, the studio plates, and the likeness-specific rig code.
- They live in the user's private package `WHY_Host_Private_Assets.zip`. Its README covers the rig, how to run it and what's missing.
- Rhubarb Lip Sync 1.13.0 (MIT) is downloaded per session from its GitHub release.
- The studio plate is 2576×1438. In 9:16 it is used as a deliberately soft-focus background (an allowed fill, → LAY-05). A native 9:16 plate would allow a sharp background.

## Known limits (future enhancements, not blockers)
- The mouth shapes are prototype quality (local edits of one close-up). A painted phoneme set would improve close shots.
- One framing and few gestures so far. Next additions: a second framing (MCU) and at least one more natural gesture.
- Telugu study labels should be natural Telugu, e.g. the voice's own phrase "బొమ్మా బొరుసు వేసి ఎంచుకున్నారు" rather than "random ఎంపిక".
- Keep secondary labels around a chart few; small phones crowd quickly.
- The lower ~350 px under the citation is the platform overlay zone. It is not filled for decoration.
