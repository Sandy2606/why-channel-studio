### CHAPTER 1 · IT CAME FROM THE STARS
[STORYTELLING · natural] So first, where does gold even come **from**? ⏸ Not from Earth. Not even from our **Sun**.
[EXPLAIN · friendly] Stars make new elements by squeezing atoms together. Hydrogen into helium, helium into carbon... all the way up to **iron**. And there, it stops. ⏸ Gold is way heavier than iron, so a normal star just **can't** make it.
[DRAMATIC · slower] Gold is made when two **dead stars**, neutron stars, smash into each other. ⏸ And in **2017**, scientists actually **watched** one of those crashes happen. **130 million** light-years away.
[AMAZED] And here's the twist. When Earth was young and molten, almost all of its gold **sank** into the core. ⏸ The gold we dig up today arrived **later**, on meteorites.
[EXPLAIN · friendly] So the gold we can actually reach is like a **thin dusting** left near the surface. ⏸ That's reason number one. It's **rare**.
[AMAZED · slower] How rare? ⏸ Every bit of gold humans have **ever** mined, in all of history, is about **222,000 tonnes**. ⏸ Melt it all into one block, and you'd get a cube about **22 metres** on each side. ⏸ Roughly the height of a **seven-storey** building. ⏸ That's it. That's **all** of it.
⏸⏸ (CHAPTER 2 card: IT LASTS FOREVER)

### CHAPTER 2 · IT LASTS FOREVER
[CASUAL · fast] Now, reason number two. ⏸ Gold basically **never dies**.
[EXPLAIN · friendly] Iron rusts. Silver turns black. Copper goes green. ⏸ But gold holds on to its electrons **so tightly** that oxygen just can't grab it. ⏸ That's why King Tut's gold mask still **shines** after more than **3,300 years**.
[AMAZED] And this matters for the price, big time. ⏸ Because gold doesn't rot, almost all the gold **ever** mined is **still around** today. ⏸ It just keeps changing hands. Coin, to necklace, to bar, to ring.
[WARM · teasing] So the gold chain your mom wears? ⏸ Some of that gold could have been a **king's coin** once. ⏸ Nobody can say for sure... but that's how gold works. It just keeps **coming back**.
⏸⏸ (CHAPTER 3 card: WHY GOLD BECAME MONEY)

### CHAPTER 3 · WHY GOLD BECAME MONEY
[CURIOUS · leaning in] Okay, but here's a question I never thought about. ⏸ Out of **everything** on Earth... why did humans pick **gold** as money?
[PLAYFUL · fast] A chemist actually answered this using the **periodic table**. ⏸ Imagine you're choosing what to use as money. ⏸ Gases? You can't carry them. ⏸ Liquids, like mercury? Nope. ⏸ Metals that catch **fire**, or react with water? Gone. ⏸ Radioactive ones? Obviously not, they'd **poison** you.
[EXPLAIN · friendly] Then you cut the common ones, like iron and copper. Too easy to find, and they corrode. ⏸ And you cut the super-rare ones. You can't make enough coins.
[REVEAL · punchy] What's left? ⏸ Basically **silver**, **platinum**... and **gold**. ⏸ Silver tarnishes. Platinum was too hard to melt for ancient people. ⏸ So gold **wins**. Rare, but not too rare. Easy to melt, easy to shape... and it **never** rots.
[WARM] That's why, for thousands of years, from Egyptian kings to our own grandmothers, people trusted **gold**.
⏸⏸ (CHAPTER 4 card: SO HARD TO GET)

### CHAPTER 4 · SO HARD TO GET
[STORYTELLING · natural] Reason number three. ⏸ Getting gold out of the ground is **brutal**.
[EXPLAIN · friendly] In a typical gold mine today, they dig and crush about **one tonne** of rock... to get roughly **one and a half grams** of gold. ⏸ A whole tonne of rock... for a piece of gold **smaller than a peppercorn**.
[AMAZED] And even with giant machines, the whole world mines only about **3,600 tonnes** a year. ⏸ That adds less than **2 percent** to all the gold that exists. ⏸ You can't just **print** more gold.
⏸⏸ (CHAPTER 5 card: WHY THE PRICE KEEPS CLIMBING)

### CHAPTER 5 · WHY THE PRICE KEEPS CLIMBING
[CURIOUS · leaning in] Okay, so gold is rare, it lasts forever, it's hard to mine. ⏸ But that was **always** true. So why has the price gone **crazy** in the last few years?
[REVEAL · slower] Here's the big one. ⏸ **Countries** are buying it.
[EXPLAIN · friendly] Central banks, the banks that run a country's money, used to buy around **470 tonnes** of gold a year. ⏸ Then from 2022 to 2024, they bought more than **1,000 tonnes**. ⏸ Every. Single. Year. ⏸ And in 2025, still about **860**.
[EXPLAIN · natural] Why? ⏸ Because when the world feels scary, wars, rising prices, countries not trusting each other... ⏸ gold is the one thing that isn't anybody's **promise**. ⏸ A government can print more money. ⏸ Nobody can print more **gold**.
[AMAZED · fast] So the price took off. ⏸ In 2019, one ounce was around **1,400 dollars**. ⏸ In January 2026, it crossed **5,000**. ⏸ It's come down since then, it's around **4,000** right now... ⏸ but that's still almost **three times** what it was in 2019.

### CHAPTER 6 · AND THEN THERE'S INDIA
[WARM · natural] And for us in India, gold isn't just an investment. ⏸ It's **family**.
[STORYTELLING · friendly] Weddings. Akshaya Tritiya. **Dhanteras**, which is coming up on **November 6th**. ⏸ Gold gets passed from mothers to daughters, from one generation to the next.
[EXPLAIN · friendly] India is one of the **two biggest** gold buyers in the whole world, along with China. ⏸ So when you see gold prices jump in the news, part of that story is happening in **our** homes.
