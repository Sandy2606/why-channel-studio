### CHAPTER 1 · WHY DOES DIWALI KEEP CHANGING ITS DATE?
6. ANNA So. ⏸ What was the first one?

   → calendar page; sun icon slides out, moon slides in
9. ANNA Your birthday follows the sun calendar, so it's the same date every year. But most of our festivals follow the moon.
10. ANNA From one new moon to the next is about **29 and a half days**. Twelve of those make only about **354 days**.
   → moon-phase strip; counter 354 vs 365
11. CHELLI That's **eleven days** short!
12. ANNA Exactly. So every year, Diwali comes about **eleven days earlier**.
   → date marker slides back on the calendar
13. CHELLI Then why doesn't it end up in **summer** one day?
14. ANNA Because every two or three years, the calendar adds **one extra month**. It's called **adhik maas**. That pulls the festivals back into place.
   → an extra month card drops in, the marker jumps forward
15. ANNA It's like a watch that runs a little fast. ⏸ Every few years, you fix the time.
16. ANNA That's why Diwali always lands between **mid-October and mid-November**.
16b. ANNA You can see the difference with two festivals you know. **Sankranti** follows the sun, so it's almost always January 14th or 15th.
   → Sankranti kite on a fixed calendar date
16c. ANNA And **Eid** follows the moon with **no** extra month. So it keeps moving about eleven days every year, through every season.
   → crescent sliding across the year, summer → winter → summer
17. CHELLI And why that **one** night?
18. ANNA Diwali is on **Amavasya**, the new-moon night of the month of Kartika. ⏸ The night with **no moon at all**.
   → sky goes dark, the moon fades; diyas light up one by one
19. ANNA The darkest night of the month… is the one night every lamp really shows.
