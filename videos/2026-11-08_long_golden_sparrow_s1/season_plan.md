**Central question:** How did a land that drew merchants from across the world end up under the growing power of a foreign trading company? Each chapter reveals another part of the answer.

**Film (aim 15–18 min; a strong 12 min beats an unfinished 18):**
- Opening: grandmother's trunk, coin and map, plus a short cold open on the epics and the Indus cities.
- Six chapters, then a Season 2 cliffhanger.
- Through-line: one piece of Indian cotton cloth, followed from field → spinner → weaver → dyer → merchant → port → ship → a European company's warehouse.
- Anna and Chelli frame the film, used sparingly.
- Period people appear as "DRAMATIZED RECONSTRUCTION".

**Shorts (updated Nov 6):**
- The story decides the runtime, up to **3:00 max**. Long ones aim to finish around 2:45–2:55; simple ones stay short.
- EN and TE may differ in length.
- Never trim needed context, and never stretch a short story.
- Each Short stands alone, has no recap, and ends with a teaser for the next episode plus "full film linked".
- Content ID: Shorts over 1:00 with an active claim can be blocked, so music, SFX and art must be cleared.
- Judge them by retention and average % viewed.
1. Was India really that rich? (the Maddison ~24% estimate, and what it does and doesn't mean)
2. The cloth the world wanted (muslin, chintz; the 1700/1721 laws described precisely, covering specific fabrics and uses, not all Indian textiles)
3. Golconda & Machilipatnam (cloth first, diamonds second)
4. Who controlled the wealth? (land revenue, zamindars, bankers, farmans; Vijayanagara, the Mughals and the Deccan kingdoms as a system, not a list of kings)
5. Europe didn't discover India (it joined an existing Indian Ocean network; monsoon winds; the pilot at Malindi)
6. The company that came to trade (Surat contact 1607, permissions 1612 per British Library records; the 1717 farman; Plassey as the Bengal turning point, not the conquest of all India)
