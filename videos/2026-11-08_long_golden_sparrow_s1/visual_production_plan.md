# The Golden Sparrow S1: visual production plan

Approved motion-graphics system, applied to every line of the English and Telugu films and the six Shorts. Timecodes are measured from the final narration mixes.

## 1. How the film moves (three visual languages)

| Style | Used for | Motion | Count (scenes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cinematic reconstruction** | People, workshops, ports, ships, battles | 2.5D depth parallax from a depth map, slow dolly, atmosphere (dust, mist, lamp flicker), wide → medium → close sequences | 30 |
| **Animated maps** | Kingdoms, trade routes, monsoon, the Company's advance, borders | One map system (Equal Earth world + regional maps, India's official outline), colour fields that change with the story, route lines that draw, camera that travels | 19 |
| **Evidence and data** | Documents, museum objects, dates, figures, flows | Document cards with a highlight sweep, the approved data kit (grid, counters, bars, people, flows), year rolls | 31 |
| Frame story and titles | Anna and Chelli, chapter cards, Season 2 card | Approved crops, parallax, title on the impact | 17 |

**Each chapter has its own motion:**
- **Ch 1:** Economic data, the approved proof-of-concept style.
- **Ch 2:** Hands and processes: macro close-ups, step-by-step process strips, and document cards for the laws.
- **Ch 3:** Port life: harbour parallax, ships and cargo, routes lighting up, and markets.
- **Ch 4:** Systems: flow diagrams with moving coins. **The famine is restrained:** slow dissolves, no SFX hits and the music drops out.
- **Ch 5:** The ocean: monsoon winds, routes, the 1498 landing, and journal quotes.
- **Ch 6 and ending:** Documents, forts and the Company map growing step by step, then the battle in heavy, still air. No action-movie cutting.

## 2. Rules carried from the approval
- **Telugu graphics:** all labels are in natural Telugu, using the glossary in section 7. Numbers stay as international numerals. Only book titles (e.g. *The World Economy*) and the museum name stay in English.
- **1700 vs 1950:** the two maps carry big labels in 9:16 (≥ 40 px), placed above the map:
  - EN: "INDIAN SUBCONTINENT · c.1700" | "INDIA · 1950 (DIFFERENT TERRITORY)"
  - TE: "భారత ఉపఖండం · సుమారు 1700" | "భారతదేశం · 1950 (వేరే భూభాగం)"
- **Figures checked:** India 24.4 % of world GDP in 1700 and 4.2 % in 1950, population share 27.4 % in 1700, GDP per head $550 (1990 int. $). All are Maddison estimates.
  - China 22.3 % and the UK 2.9 % (1700) come from the same tables. They are standard citations of Maddison 2001, Table B-20; I could not open the OECD PDF to re-read them this round, so a page check is noted in the source file.
  - Geographic definitions: Maddison's 1700 "India" is the undivided subcontinent, and his 1950 figure is the Republic of India.
- **Mobile safe areas (9:16, 1080×1920):**
  - Keep key text inside x 70–900 and y 220–1500.
  - Nothing important below y 1500 (Shorts title and description) or right of x 900 between y 900 and 1650 (like and share buttons).
  - Minimum sizes: labels 34 px, source lines 28 px, territory labels 40 px. In 16:9: labels 26 px, sources 22 px.
- **Sensitive scenes:**
  - Caste (1.7): show the system, not ranked people.
  - Child labour (3.7): keep it wide and distant.
  - Famine (4.9–4.10): no bodies, slow fades, silence.
  - Battle (7.2–7.3): no gore, labelled DRAMATIZED.
  - Every reconstruction carries a "DRAMATIZED RECONSTRUCTION" tag the first time it appears in each chapter.
- **Separate compositions:** 16:9 and 9:16 are laid out separately (maps, text, subject placement). The same art is reused, never just centre-cropped.

## 3. Scene by scene

Each scene lists:
- **EN / TE timecodes:** from the final mixes.
- **Picture and animation:** what is on screen and how it moves.
- **Camera:** how the frame moves.
- **Sync:** the narration cue that triggers each move.
- **On-screen text:** in English and Telugu.
- **Assets:** IDs from section 5.
- **Licence:** where each asset comes from and its rights status.
- **Reuse:** across languages, aspect ratios and Shorts.

## 6. Production order
1. Telugu localisation and the 9:16 size and safe-area rules go into the shared graphics kit. This is done once and applies everywhere.
2. The prompt pack v2 page for the 30 new illustrations: same phone workflow, with ★ shots using the character sheet.
3. In parallel, I collect the public-domain, open-access and CC images and log each URL and licence in `credits.md`.
4. I build the remaining maps: Coromandel coast, Indian Ocean, Deccan, and the Company map.
5. Assemble chapter by chapter, render 16:9 and 9:16 in both languages, and run the QA stills sheet per chapter.
6. Cut the six Shorts from the 9:16 film layers, adding burned-in captions.
