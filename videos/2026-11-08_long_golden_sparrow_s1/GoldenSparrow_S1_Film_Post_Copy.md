## ENGLISH · YouTube

**Title**
The Golden Sparrow: How India Made a Quarter of the World's Wealth (and Lost It) | Season 1

**Description**
In 1721, Britain passed a law banning people from wearing certain printed cotton fabrics. Why? 🧵

We follow one painted cotton cloth from a Deccan field in c.1680, through a pit loom and a dyer's vat, to the port of Machilipatnam, and all the way to a London auction. Along the way, we find out whether India was really the "golden sparrow", who actually held the wealth, and how a company that came asking permission ended up collecting the taxes.

🌾 By one estimate (Angus Maddison), India produced about 24% of the world's output in 1700. That came mostly from its huge population and its manufacturing, not from everyone being rich.
🧵 Indian dyers made colours that didn't wash out. Europe tried for decades to copy them.
🚫 France banned printed calicoes in 1686. Britain's 1721 Act banned wearing them from Christmas 1722, until 1774.
⚓ Machilipatnam, Golconda's sea gate, drew ships from Persia, Arabia, Burma and Southeast Asia. The Dutch and the English set up trade there too.
💎 Golconda's diamonds made the legends, but cloth filled the ships.
🌊 Vasco da Gama didn't discover India. He joined an ocean that had been busy for centuries.
🌶️ Spices brought Europeans east. Indian cloth became the big prize.
🏴 The East India Company asked permission for 150 years. Then came Plassey (1757), Buxar (1764) and the right to collect Bengal's revenue (1765).

What happens when a company that once needed permission to trade starts collecting the taxes? That's Season 2.
