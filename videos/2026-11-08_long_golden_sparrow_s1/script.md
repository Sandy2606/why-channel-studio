[STORY · reverent] India's story is older than its kingdoms. ⏸ It's the land of the **Ramayana** and the **Mahabharata**, epics told and retold for more than two thousand years. ⏸ For millions of people they're **itihasa**: "so it happened." ⏸ And Ayodhya, Kurukshetra and Hastinapur still carry their names today.
   → Painted scenes from the epics (old manuscript style) dissolve into the same places today. Small caption: "COMPOSED AND RETOLD OVER MANY CENTURIES · SCHOLARS DATE THE TEXTS c. 5th CENTURY BCE – 4th CENTURY CE".

6. [SETUP · clear] But this film is about a later chapter: ⏸ the 250 years **before** the British took power. ⏸ **1500 to 1757.** ⏸ Six chapters, one story.

### CHAPTER 1 · WAS INDIA REALLY THAT RICH?
7. [CURIOUS] **CHELLI:** People call India "the golden sparrow." ⏸ Was it really that rich?
8. [EXPLAINING] Let me show you a number. ⏸ The economic historian **Angus Maddison** tried to estimate every country's economy going back centuries. ⏸ His estimate for the year **1700**: ⏸ India made about **24 percent**, almost **a quarter**, of everything produced in the world.
   → A world pie chart fills in: India ≈ 24%. Source chip "MADDISON · HISTORICAL ESTIMATE".
9. [CONTRAST] Britain in 1700? ⏸ About **3 percent**.
   → A tiny Britain slice pops in beside it.
10. [HONEST · lighter] Now, an honest note. ⏸ India was big, and it had **a huge population**. ⏸ So a large economy didn't mean every family was rich. ⏸ Most people were farmers, and many were poor.
    → The pie turns into crowds of people; a farmer ploughs. Caption: "BIG ECONOMY ≠ EVERYONE RICH".
11. [BUILD] But here's what matters: ⏸ India **made** things the world couldn't make anywhere else. ⏸ And the world paid for them in **silver and gold**.
    → Ships arrive with chests of silver, which pour onto the map.
12. [TEASE] **CHELLI:** What could be so special that people sailed across oceans for it?
    **ANNA:** ⏸ Something you're wearing right now.

### CHAPTER 2 · THE CLOTH THE WORLD WANTED
13. [STORY · intimate] Meet **Rahim**. ⏸ He's a weaver in Dhaka, in Bengal, in the 1600s.
    → Rahim at a pit loom, light through the window. Tag: "ILLUSTRATIVE CHARACTER".
14. [WONDER] He weaves **muslin**. ⏸ Ordinary cloth has about **40 to 80 threads** in an inch. ⏸ The finest Dhaka muslin had **800 to 1,200**.
    → Macro split screen: coarse weave vs an impossibly fine weave. Counter: 80 → 1,200.
15. [WONDER] It was so light, people called it **"woven air."** ⏸ Travellers wrote that a whole sheet could be pulled **through a ring**.
    → A sheer cloth is drawn through a finger ring in slow motion.
16. [EXPLAINING] And it came from a cotton plant, **phuti karpas**, that grew only along one river near Dhaka. ⏸ The Mughal emperors had a grade made **only for royalty**.
    → The Meghna riverbank; a cotton boll; a Mughal court painting (evidence chip "MUGHAL COURT").
17. [BUILD] It wasn't only Bengal. ⏸ Gujarat, the Coromandel coast, Machilipatnam: ⏸ Indian **cotton, chintz and kalamkari** were shipped to Persia, Africa, Southeast Asia and Europe.
    → Bolts of cloth fly along trade routes on the map. A real museum chintz panel (open access).
18. [TWIST] Indian cloth became so popular in England ⏸ that England **banned** it. ⏸ Laws in **1700** and **1721**, the **Calico Acts**, stopped people from wearing printed Indian cotton, because English weavers couldn't compete.
    → Clipping of the 1721 Act; an angry London crowd tearing a calico dress. Source chip "CALICO ACTS 1700, 1721".
19. [TEASE] **CHELLI:** So cloth made India rich?
    **ANNA:** ⏸ Cloth… ⏸ and something that sparkled. ⏸ And it came from **our** side of India.

### CHAPTER 3 · THE DIAMOND LANDS
20. [STORY] Meet **Venkanna**. ⏸ He digs near the **Krishna river**, at **Kollur**, in today's Andhra Pradesh, in the 1600s.
    → Pits along the Krishna, workers with baskets, a watchtower. Tag: "ILLUSTRATIVE CHARACTER".
21. [WONDER] Here's a fact most people don't know: ⏸ until the **1720s**, India was the world's **only** known source of diamonds.
    → A world map with a single glowing point: the Krishna valley. Caption "UNTIL DIAMONDS WERE FOUND IN BRAZIL (1720s)".
22. [EXPLAINING] These were the **Golconda diamonds**, ruled by the **Qutb Shahi** kings who built **Hyderabad** and the **Charminar**. ⏸ Accounts say tens of thousands of people worked these mines at their peak.
    → Golconda fort, Charminar (photo), a tray of rough diamonds.
23. [STORY] A French gem dealer, **Tavernier**, travelled here again and again and called them the purest diamonds in the world. ⏸ Many famous stones are traced to these lands, ⏸ and many accounts trace even the **Kohinoor** here, though historians still debate exactly where it was found.
    → Tavernier's book page (public domain) as a clipping; the Kohinoor silhouette with a "?" stamp "ORIGIN DEBATED".
24. [BUILD] And the goods left through **Machilipatnam**, a busy port of Golconda. ⏸ The Dutch opened a trading house there in **1605**. ⏸ The English, in **1611**.
    → Map zoom to Machilipatnam; Dutch and English flags appear on two warehouses.
25. [TEASE] **CHELLI:** Wait. ⏸ Who **controlled** all this wealth?
    **ANNA:** ⏸ Kings. ⏸ And they were powerful.

### CHAPTER 4 · THE KINGS
26. [STORY · grand] In the 1500s, in the south, stood **Vijayanagara**, at Hampi. ⏸ A Portuguese visitor, **Domingo Paes**, wrote that the city looked **"as large as Rome."**
    → Hampi ruins (photo) → a painted reconstruction of the living city; the Paes quote as a clipping.
27. [STORY] Its greatest king, **Krishnadevaraya**, was also a poet, ⏸ and he wrote in **Telugu**.
    → A king writing on palm leaf; Telugu script glows.
28. [TURN] But in **1565**, after the battle of **Talikota**, the city was sacked and never recovered.
    → The city burns into ruins; the map redraws the borders.
29. [EXPLAINING] In the north rose the **Mughals**. ⏸ By around **1700**, their empire covered most of the subcontinent. ⏸ They collected taxes, built roads and cities, and spent lavishly on art.
    → Mughal territory spreads on the map; a Mughal painting of a court; Taj Mahal (photo).
30. [HONEST] Their history has both sides: ⏸ great buildings and trade networks, ⏸ and wars, temple destruction in some campaigns, and heavy taxes. ⏸ Historians argue about it, and we'll show you the evidence in another season.
    → A split card: "BUILT · TRADED" / "CONQUERED · DESTROYED · TAXED", with a "SEASON 4" chip.
31. [TURN · ominous] And when the emperor **Aurangzeb** died in **1707**, ⏸ the empire started breaking into pieces. ⏸ Nawabs, Marathas, Nizams… ⏸ many powers, and many rivals.
    → The single Mughal colour on the map cracks into many colours.
32. [TEASE] **CHELLI:** And while India was splitting up…
    **ANNA:** ⏸ Europe had been sailing towards it for **200 years**.

### CHAPTER 5 · WHY EUROPE SAILED
33. [STORY] Go back to **1492**. ⏸ **Columbus** sailed west, looking for **India**.
    → A caravel on a dark ocean; the 1492 globe.
34. [TWIST · playful] He landed in America, ⏸ was sure he'd reached the Indies, ⏸ and called the people there **"Indians."** ⏸ That name stuck for 500 years.
    → The map flips to the Americas; an "INDIA?" label gets crossed out.
35. [EXPLAINING] Why did Europe want India so badly? ⏸ **Pepper, cloth, diamonds.** ⏸ Spices that cost a fortune in Europe grew here.
    → Pepper vines in Kerala; a European scale where pepper outweighs gold coins.
36. [STORY] In **1498**, the Portuguese sailor **Vasco da Gama** reached **Calicut**, in Kerala, by sailing around Africa.
    → His route draws around Africa to Calicut. A drawn Portuguese sailor steps ashore.
37. [BUILD] After him came the **Dutch**, the **English**, the **French**, ⏸ all racing for the same thing: **trade with India.**
    → Four flags race across the Indian Ocean.
38. [TEASE] **CHELLI:** And the English?
    **ANNA:** ⏸ They didn't send an army. ⏸ They sent a **company**.

### CHAPTER 6 · THE COMPANY THAT CAME TO TRADE
39. [STORY] On the last day of **1600**, Queen Elizabeth gave a group of London merchants a charter: ⏸ the **East India Company**.
    → The charter document (public domain) as a clipping, dated 31 Dec 1600.
40. [STORY] In **1608**, its ship reached **Surat**. ⏸ In **1615**, its ambassador **Thomas Roe** stood in the court of the Mughal emperor **Jahangir**, ⏸ asking for permission **to trade**.
    → A Company clerk with a ledger at Surat; Roe bowing at Jahangir's court (painting).
41. [BUILD] It looked harmless. ⏸ A trading house in **Madras**. ⏸ Then **Bombay**. ⏸ Then **Calcutta**. ⏸ Slowly they added **forts**, **soldiers** and **guns**.
    → Pins drop on the map: Madras 1639, Bombay 1668, Calcutta 1690; each warehouse grows walls.
42. [TENSE] Then came **June 23, 1757**. ⏸ **Plassey**, in Bengal.
    → The screen darkens; rain on a mango grove; a date stamp.
43. [TENSE · numbers] The Nawab of Bengal, **Siraj-ud-Daulah**, had an army of about **50,000**. ⏸ The Company's commander, **Robert Clive**, had about **3,000**.
    → Two armies as dot grids: 50,000 vs 3,000.
44. [TWIST · slow] But Clive had made a secret deal. ⏸ The Nawab's own commander, **Mir Jafar**, was promised the throne. ⏸ On the day of the battle, his troops **barely fought**.
    → A sealed letter passes between hands; Mir Jafar's division stands still on the map.
45. [WEIGHT] The Nawab lost. ⏸ Mir Jafar was made Nawab, ⏸ and the Company was paid about **two crore rupees**.
    → The 1760s painting of Clive and Mir Jafar after Plassey (public domain) as evidence.
46. [REVELATION · slow] A company that came **to trade** ⏸ had just decided **who would rule Bengal**.
    → The Company's flag rises over Bengal on the map.

### అధ్యాయం 1 · India నిజంగా అంత ధనిక దేశమా?
7. [CURIOUS] **CHELLI:** India ని "బంగారు పిచ్చుక" అంటారు. ⏸ నిజంగా అంత ధనిక దేశమా?
8. [EXPLAINING] ఒక సంఖ్య చూపిస్తా. ⏸ ఆర్థిక చరిత్రకారుడు **Angus Maddison** శతాబ్దాల వెనక్కి వెళ్లి ప్రతి దేశ ఆర్థిక వ్యవస్థను అంచనా వేశారు. ⏸ **1700** సంవత్సరానికి ఆయన అంచనా: ⏸ ప్రపంచంలో తయారైన మొత్తంలో దాదాపు **24 శాతం**, అంటే దాదాపు **నాలుగో వంతు**, India దే.

### అధ్యాయం 3 · వజ్రాల నేల
