# Short 3 · Wed Oct 28 · "Why is gold yellow?" · tag GOLD · 3/6 · friend-to-friend tone

Sources:
- Fourmilab, "What Gives Gold that Mellow Glow?": gold's inner electrons move at roughly half the speed of light; relativity makes them heavier and pulls the s orbitals in; that moves gold's main absorption (5d→6s) from ultraviolet into blue. Silver's matching jump stays in the ultraviolet, so silver reflects all visible light. https://fourmilab.ch/documents/golden_glow/
- ZME Science, "Gold's color is due to relativity". https://zmescience.com/feature-post/gold-color-relativity
- Calvo et al., Angewandte Chemie 2013: relativity is why mercury is liquid at room temperature (bonus line).

## EN
[HOOK · curious, fast] Look at your keys, your phone, your spoon. ⏸ Almost **every** metal is silver or grey. ⏸ So why is gold... **yellow**?
[CASUAL · friendly] Honestly, I thought it was just one of those things. ⏸ Turns out the answer is **Einstein**.
[EXPLAIN · building] A gold atom is **huge**. 79 protons in the middle, pulling **really** hard on its electrons. ⏸ So hard that the inner ones are zooming around at about **half the speed of light**.
[AMAZED] And when anything moves that fast, Einstein's **relativity** kicks in. ⏸ The electrons get heavier... and get pulled in **closer**.
[EXPLAIN · simple] That tiny change shifts which light gold **soaks up**. ⏸ Gold eats a bit of the **blue** light... and bounces back the rest. ⏸ Take the blue out of white light, and what's left looks **warm and yellow**.
[REVEAL · punchy] Silver has the **same** setup. But in silver, that jump happens in light we **can't even see**. ⏸ So it bounces back **everything**, and looks silver.
[MIND-BLOWN · slower] Which means... ⏸ without relativity, gold would look **just like silver**.
[BONUS · playful] Oh, and the same effect is a big part of why **mercury** is a liquid. ⏸ Physics is wild.
