## ENGLISH · YouTube Short

**Title**
It Came Asking Permission. Then It Collected the Taxes 🏛️ #shorts

**Description**
The East India Company came to India asking permission. A hundred and fifty years later, it helped put a Nawab on Bengal's throne. How? 🤔

📜 1608: William Hawkins reached Surat and travelled to Emperor Jahangir's court. After English ships beat a Portuguese fleet off Swally (1612), a deal was confirmed by the emperor in early 1613. Sir Thomas Roe stayed at court from 1615 to 1619, pressing for better terms.
⚔️ 1686: the Company went to war against Aurangzeb… and lost. It had to beg the emperor's pardon, pay a heavy fine and promise to behave. In 1690 it set up a new base in Bengal: Calcutta.
🧾 1717: Emperor Farrukhsiyar's farman gave duty-free trade in Bengal for a fixed 3,000 rupees a year, with passes called dastaks. Company men soon misused the passes for their own private trade.
🪖 1740s–50s: the French and English companies fought in South India, trained Indian sepoys in European drill and backed rival claimants to the thrones of the Carnatic and Hyderabad. The French did it first.
🏹 1757, Plassey: about 3,000 Company troops (about 2,100 of them Indian sepoys) faced an army of around 50,000. Much of it never fought: Mir Jafar's cavalry stood aside, and the Company helped put him on Bengal's throne.
🗺️ Plassey didn't conquer India. But after Buxar (1764), the Mughal emperor granted the Company the right to collect the revenue of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa (1765): the diwani.
🪙 For a century and a half the Company had shipped silver to India to pay for its cloth. After 1765, it could increasingly pay with revenue collected in India itself.

What happens when a company that once needed permission to trade… starts collecting the taxes? That's where Season 2 begins.
