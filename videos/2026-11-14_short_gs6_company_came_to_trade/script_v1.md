## ENGLISH
1. [HOOK · fast] It came to India asking **permission**. ⏸ A hundred and fifty years later, it helped put a **Nawab** on Bengal's **throne**. (reuse S6_hook) → chip 1/3
2. [STEADY · dates clear] In **1608**, William **Hawkins** reached Surat and travelled to the court of the emperor **Jahangir**. ⏸ In **1612**, English ships beat a Portuguese fleet off **Swally**, near Surat, and Mughal officials took notice. ⏸ A deal with Surat's governor was confirmed by the emperor in early **1613**. ⏸ And from **1615 to 1619**, King James's ambassador, Sir **Thomas Roe**, stayed at the court, pressing for better terms. (reuse 6.3)
3. [STORY · a little wry] And the Company had already tried **force** once. ⏸ In **1686**, it went to war against the Mughal emperor **Aurangzeb**… and **lost**. ⏸ Its envoys had to beg the emperor's **pardon**, pay a heavy fine, and promise to behave. ⏸ Only then could it trade again. And in **1690**, it set up a new base in Bengal: **Calcutta**. (reuse 6.8b)
4. [PRECISE] In **1717**, Emperor **Farrukhsiyar** granted it a **farman**: duty-free trade in Bengal, for a fixed **3,000 rupees** a year, with passes called **dastaks**. ⏸ Company men soon **misused** those passes for their own private trade. (reuse 6.9)
5. CHELLI [puzzled] Wait… how does a **trading** company end up with an **army**? (NEW) → chip 2/3
6. [DARKER] Then came the **wars**. ⏸ In the 1740s and 50s, the **French** and **English** companies fought each other in South India. They trained Indian soldiers, called **sepoys**, in European drill. ⏸ And they backed rival claimants to the thrones of the **Carnatic** and **Hyderabad**. The French did it first. (reuse 6.10)
7. [OMINOUS · slow] The traders had learned something **dangerous**. ⏸ Fighting the Mughals had **failed**. But choosing **rulers**… paid. (reuse 6.12)
8. [TENSE] In Bengal, the young Nawab **Siraj-ud-Daulah** was angry: the Company was building fortifications and abusing its passes. ⏸ In **1756**, he seized **Calcutta**. ⏸ The Company sent an army from **Madras**. (reuse 6.13)
9. [DRAMATIC] On **23 June 1757**, in Bengal, about **three thousand** Company troops faced an army of around **fifty thousand**. (reuse 7.2) → chip 3/3
10. CHELLI [shocked] Three thousand… against **fifty** thousand? (NEW)
11. [LOW · deliberate] Much of that army **never fought**. ⏸ Its commander, **Mir Jafar**, had made a **secret deal**… and the Company helped put him on Bengal's throne. (reuse 7.3)
12. [BUILDING] Plassey didn't conquer **India**. ⏸ But at **Buxar** in **1764**, the Company won again. ⏸ And in **1765**, the Mughal emperor granted it the right to collect the **revenue** of Bengal, Bihar and Orissa. (reuse 7.5)
13. [BRIDGE] For a century and a half, the Company had shipped **silver** to India to pay for its cloth. ⏸ After **1765**, it could increasingly pay with **revenue** collected in India itself. (reuse B6)
14. [FINAL QUESTION · slow] What happens when a company that once needed **permission** to trade… starts collecting the **taxes**? (reuse 7.9)
15. [TEASE] That's where **Season 2** begins. ⏸ The full film is linked. (reuse S6_tease)
