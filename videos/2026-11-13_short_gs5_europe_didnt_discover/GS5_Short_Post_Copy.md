## ENGLISH · YouTube Short

**Title**
Vasco da Gama Didn't Discover India. Who Showed Him the Way? ⚓ #shorts

**Description**
Vasco da Gama didn't discover India. The ocean he sailed into had been busy for centuries, and someone had to show him the way. 🧭

🌊 Indian, Arab, Persian, African, Chinese and Malay traders were crossing the Indian Ocean long before 1498. At Malacca, Gujarati merchants were among the biggest traders.
🌬️ Their secret was the monsoon: it blows one way for half the year and back the other way for the rest. Sailors timed whole voyages around it.
🧭 At Malindi in 1498, da Gama took on an experienced pilot who guided his ships to Calicut. The crew's journal calls him a "Christian", but it used the same word for the Hindus of Calicut, so it tells us little about his faith. Later Portuguese chroniclers describe a navigator from Gujarat, Malemo Canaqua; "Malemo" is a title (master navigator), not a name. The famous story that it was Ibn Majid is considered unlikely by most historians. So the pilot was most likely from Gujarat, in India.
😄 In Calicut, two merchants from Tunis asked, "What brought you here?" The answer: "Christians and spices." The king's officials laughed at da Gama's gifts.
🌍 Europe hadn't found a new world here. It had joined an old one.
⚔️ The Portuguese used treaties and cannons. A century later, the English sought trading permissions, and they used force too.
