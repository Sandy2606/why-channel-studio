## Direction in one line
**One map, one line of light.** The video opens on a real European map from 1502. It then turns into an accurate map of an Indian Ocean that is *already full* of routes. Da Gama's single line plugs into that network, and a pilot from the network carries it across. Every scene adds a route, a pin or a document to the same ocean, so the story builds connection by connection instead of cutting between unrelated pictures.

## Look (same design system as GS 1–4)
- **Palette:** ink #0A0A0A, cream #F4F1EA and amber #FFB224, with parchment, antique gold #C4A05C and muted sea-blue for water.
- **Archival styling:** museum-label annotations (ink plate, thin gold rule, kicker + title, leader line to the exact spot) and ink cartouches with a gold double rule. No stamps, split-flap boards, neon or red.
- **Full screen, no masked bottom** (rule from GS 4/6). Captions sit in the caption lane on a calm part of the picture, with text shadow only.
- **Sharp pictures only:**
  - GS illustrations G22 and G23 (1672×941) appear as native-pixel panels, scale ≤ 1.0, never stretched.
  - Full-screen use is only for sources tall enough: the Cantino planisphere (10,643×4,998, cropped at ≤ 1.0) and the Roteiro manuscript (2,335×3,184).
  - Maps are drawn at 1080×1920. Laplacian sharpness is checked per scene.
