4. [EXPLAIN] First, the **spinner**. Usually a woman, spinning at home between her other work. ⏸ It took **many** spinners to keep **one** loom busy. (reuse 2.2)
   → spinning wheel → many spinners : one loom.
5. [EXPLAIN] Then the **weaver**. A pit loom, the whole family working. ⏸ Weavers were often **paid in advance** by brokers… and the broker usually set the **price**. ⏸ World-class skill. Very little **bargaining power**. (reuse 2.3)
   → loom (warp/weft/shuttle) → broker money arrow → price tag.
6. CHELLI [curious] Then what made it **better** than Europe's cloth? (NEW)
   → chip 2/3 THE SECRET.
7. [REVEAL] Then came the real secret: the **dyer**. (reuse 2.4)
8. [EXPLAIN] European dyers could colour cloth. ⏸ Indian dyers made colours that **didn't wash out**. (reuse 2.5)
   → wash test: European colour fades, Indian stays.
9. [EXPLAIN · step by step] With a pen called a **kalam**, they drew the design in **mordants**, substances that fix dye to cotton. ⏸ Then came **reds** from the chay root, **blue** from indigo, and **wax** to protect some areas. Step after step, for **weeks**. (reuse 2.6)
   → 4-step process with progress (mordant → red → wax → blue); palampore as "illustrative reconstruction".
10. [EXPLAIN] Europe tried for **decades** to copy it. ⏸ In the 1740s, a French Jesuit priest named **Coeurdoux** sent home detailed descriptions of how it was done. (reuse 2.8)
    → letter evidence flash.
11. [WONDER] And in Bengal, weavers made muslin so fine, people called it **woven air**. (reuse 2.9)
12. [BUILD] In Europe, people went **crazy** for it: bright, light, washable cotton. ⏸ And that's when the **bans** began. (reuse 2.10)
13. CHELLI [surprised] **Bans?** Who banned it? (NEW)
    → chip 3/3 THE BANS.
14. [EXPLAIN · timeline] France banned printed calicoes in **1686**. ⏸ England's **1700** Act stopped printed and painted calicoes from Asia being used at home. They could still be **re-exported**. (reuse 2.11)
    → timeline 1686 · 1700.
15. [EXPLAIN] Then the **1721** Act went further. ⏸ From Christmas **1722**, it banned **wearing or using** printed, painted, stained or dyed calicoes, whether printed abroad or in Britain, with a few **exceptions**. (reuse 2.12)
    → 1721/1722 on timeline; Act page evidence flash.
16. [PUNCH] It stayed in force until **1774**. (reuse 2.13)
17. [EXPLAIN] These laws protected Britain's **wool and silk** weavers. ⏸ And they tell you how **good** Indian cotton was. (reuse 2.14)
18. [EXPLAIN · quick] Indian cloth also bought **spices** in Southeast Asia and travelled into **West Africa**. ⏸ Even the English words **calico**, from Calicut, and **chintz** came from India. (reuse 2.15)
    → trade arrows; word cards CALICO ← CALICUT, CHINTZ.
