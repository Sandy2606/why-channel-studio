## ENGLISH · YouTube Short

**Title**
Why Did Britain Ban Wearing Indian Printed Cotton? The 1721 Law 🧵 #shorts

**Description**
In 1721, Britain passed a law banning people from wearing certain printed cotton fabrics. Why? 🤔

🧶 Spinners: usually women, spinning at home. It took many spinners to keep one loom busy.
🪡 Weavers: pit looms, whole families at work, often paid in advance by brokers who set the price. World-class skill, little bargaining power.
🎨 Dyers: with a kalam pen they drew the design in mordants, then added reds from the chay root, blue from indigo and wax resist, step after step for weeks. Colours that held through washing.
📜 Europe tried for decades to copy it. In the 1740s, the French Jesuit Coeurdoux sent home detailed descriptions.
⚖️ The bans: France banned printed calicoes in 1686. England's 1700 Act stopped Asian printed and painted calicoes being used at home (re-export was allowed). The 1721 Act, in force from 25 December 1722, banned wearing or using printed, painted, stained or dyed calicoes, with some exceptions. It lasted until 1774.
🌍 Indian cloth also bought spices in Southeast Asia and travelled to West Africa. Even "calico" (from Calicut) and "chintz" came from India.
