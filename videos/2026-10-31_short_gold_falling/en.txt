In January, gold hit about $5,590 an ounce. This week? It slipped below $4,000.
That's almost 30 percent gone in nine months. So what happened to the "safest" thing you can own?
Reason one. Gold doesn't pay you anything. A bank FD pays interest. Gold just… sits there.
So when interest rates go up, gold looks less attractive. And in September, America's Fed raised rates for the first time since 2023.
Reason two. Oil jumped on US-Iran tension. Oil up means prices up… and even more fear of rate hikes.
Reason three. When stock markets fall, big investors need cash, fast. And the easiest thing to sell? Gold.
The fast money that rushed in back in January… is now rushing out.
But here's what nobody's talking about. In September, central banks bought about 63 tonnes of gold. The most in almost two years.
So the traders are selling… and the central banks are buying.
And this isn't new. In 2011, gold set a record. It took nine years to beat it, in 2020.
Still, since 2006, it's up about 17 times in rupees.
Gold goes up and down like everything else. That's why people who hold it… think in years, not weeks.
But gold still has a few secrets. Why is an Olympic gold medal… mostly silver? Andhra Pradesh just opened a new gold mine. So why does India still buy almost all its gold from abroad?
