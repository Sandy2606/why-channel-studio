## Fact checks (sources checked Nov 1, 2026)
- Composite made in 2003 by NOAA scientist Chris Elvidge from DMSP (US Defense Meteorological Satellite Program) OLS data; white = before 1992, blue = 1992, green = 1998, red = 2003; shows growth, not Diwali. Snopes (Nov 4, 2021), EarthSky (Nov 6, 2018).
- DMSP OLS "VIS" band detects clouds illuminated by moonlight, and also city lights. NASA SVS 2396.
- Real image: VIIRS day-night band on Suomi NPP, Nov 12, 2012 (Diwali began Nov 11, 2012); image brightened for contrast; "any extra light produced during Diwali is so subtle that it is likely imperceptible when observed from space." NASA Earth Observatory 79682.
- Suomi NPP orbit ≈ 824 km; day-night band 750 m per pixel. UW–Madison SSEC "VIIRS Basics".
- Line 9 "orange and white" describes NASA's natural-colour night images; line 12 is an explanation, not a measurement.
