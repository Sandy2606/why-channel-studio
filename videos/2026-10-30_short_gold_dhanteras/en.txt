So why do Indians trust gold more than a bank? It's not just tradition. It's math.
Say you put 10,000 rupees into gold in 2006. Today, it's worth about 1.77 lakh. Nearly 18 times.
The same 10,000 in a bank FD, even at a steady 7 percent a year? About 38,000.
Here's the hidden reason. Gold is priced in dollars. In 2006, one dollar cost about 45 rupees. Today, about 95.
So every time the rupee gets weaker, your gold gets stronger. It quietly protects your savings.
And when you need cash fast? Pledge your gold, and the loan comes in minutes, with very little paperwork.
That's why gold loans are now India's second-biggest kind of retail loan, after home loans. About 16 lakh crore rupees.
