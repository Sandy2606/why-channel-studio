## ENGLISH · YouTube Short

**Title**
India Made 1/4 of the World's Output. Who Got the Money? 💰 #shorts

**Description**
India made almost a quarter of the world's output (c.1700, Maddison estimate). So why did so many of the people who made it stay poor? 🤔

🌾 The farmer paid land revenue. Historians estimate the assessed demand was often a third to a half of the crop. It varied by place and time, and was often paid in cash.
🏛️ Collectors and local lords took their cut. The state spent on armies and courts. Bankers lent to everyone.
📜 Foreign trading companies sought royal orders, farmans, for trading rights and privileges.
🏦 In Surat, Virji Vora lent money to the English Company again and again; its own records called him the richest merchant of his time. Merchants and rulers needed each other.
👑 Kingdoms rose and fell, the Deccan sultanates held the middle, and the Mughal empire pushed south. Under Akbar, Todar Mal measured the land and set revenue by survey. Kings came and went; the system lasted.
🥀 When the rains failed, wealth at the top did not protect the people at the bottom. The famine of 1630–32 struck Gujarat and the Deccan; Peter Mundy saw it. Death tolls are historical estimates, in the millions. Many weavers died or fled.
⚓ Land brought rulers revenue, but India's ports connected them to trade across the seas.
