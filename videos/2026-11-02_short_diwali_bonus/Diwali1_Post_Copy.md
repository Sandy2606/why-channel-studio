## ENGLISH · YouTube Short

**Title:** Is Your Diwali Bonus a Gift… or the LAW? (₹21,000 Rule) #shorts

**Description:**
For millions of workers, the Diwali bonus isn't a gift. It's the law. And one number decides it: ₹21,000. 🪔

Correction first: in Sunday's full video I said the bonus comes under the Payment of Bonus Act, 1965. Since 21 Nov 2025 that Act is part of the Code on Wages, 2019. Sorry about that. The rules below are current.

👷 Ravi packs boxes at a 50-person warehouse. His wages (basic + DA) are ₹18,000 a month. That's ₹21,000 or less, so if he worked at least 30 days, his company must pay a bonus. Old law and new law: same answer.
💻 Arjun earns ₹45,000. That's above ₹21,000, so the bonus law doesn't cover him. Any Diwali bonus he gets comes from his contract or company policy.
🧮 The bonus isn't counted on your full salary. It's counted on the minimum wage for your job (or ₹7,000, whichever is higher). If Ravi's minimum wage is ₹12,000: 8.33% for 12 months ≈ ₹12,000 minimum, and up to 20% ≈ ₹28,800 when the company has enough allocable surplus.
📋 What changed in the new law:
1. The bonus must be paid into your bank account (the old law said cash).
2. It generally applies from 20 workers. Under the old law, any factory could be covered, even one with 12 workers.
3. The ₹21,000 limit is now set by notification. The Centre kept it at ₹21,000 on 25 Aug 2026.
4. A dismissal after a sexual-harassment conviction now also takes away the bonus (along with fraud, violence and theft).
📅 The company has 8 months after the year ends to pay, usually 30 November. Didn't get it? You can file a claim with the labour authority. The claim period is generally 3 years.
