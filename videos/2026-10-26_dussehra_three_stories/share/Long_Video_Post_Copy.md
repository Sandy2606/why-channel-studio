## 🇬🇧 YouTube (English channel)

**Title**
Dussehra Is NOT One Story. It's Three.

*(Alt for A/B test: "Why Is Dussehra Celebrated So Differently Across India?")*

**Description**
On one single day, India burns a 100-foot Ravana, carries Durga into the river, parades a 750 kg golden howdah, and swaps Jammi leaves as gold. How can that be ONE festival? Because Dussehra isn't one story. It's at least three.

🏹 Story 1: Rama defeats Ravana. Tradition places the victory on Vijayadashami, which is why North India has Ramlila and Ravana Dahan.
🔱 Story 2: Durga defeats Mahishasura after nine nights (the Devi Mahatmya, Markandeya Purana). His boon said no man or god could kill him; a goddess could.
🌳 Story 3: the Pandavas hide their weapons in the Shami (Jammi) tree, and tradition links Arjuna taking them back with Vijayadashami. That's why we worship the Jammi tree and why Ayudha Puja exists.
🐘 Mysuru: Chamundeshwari's festival, but the Jambu Savari ends at Bannimantap… at the Shami tree.
🏛️ Hampi: Domingo Paes described a nine-day feast in about 1520. The platform he called the "House of Victory" still stands: the Mahanavami Dibba.
🎭 Ramnagar's Ramlila runs 31 days and the whole town is the stage. UNESCO listed Ramlila in 2008, and it travelled with indentured labourers to Trinidad, Guyana, Fiji, Mauritius and Suriname.
🪔 Kolkata's Durga Puja (UNESCO 2021), Gujarat's Garba (UNESCO 2023), Telangana's Bathukamma, Kullu, Bastar (about 75 days) and Nepal's Dashain (15 days).

Every villain began with power. Power wasn't their problem. What they did with it was.
