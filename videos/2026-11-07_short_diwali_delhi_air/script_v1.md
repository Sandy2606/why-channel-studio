## ENGLISH
1. [serious] Every year, right after Diwali, Delhi wakes up inside a grey haze. … Most people blame the crackers. … They're only part of the story.
   → frame 0: Delhi skyline (India Gate silhouette) half-hidden in grey haze at dawn; a few burnt cracker shells on a balcony rail in the foreground; title "WHY DELHI CHOKES"
2. Last Diwali, an analysis of government monitoring data found PM2.5 averaged about 488 micrograms in the 24 hours after the festival… more than three times the level just before.
   → bar 156.6 → 488 (µg/m³), "×3"; evidence flash (Climate Trends analysis of CPCB data, PTI report)
3. India's own 24-hour limit is 60. … That's about eight times over.
   → a short line at 60 labelled "INDIA'S 24-HR LIMIT"; the 488 bar towers over it; "≈ 8×"
4. So yes, crackers cause a spike. … But why does the smoke stay?
   → smoke puffs rise… and stop, pressing against an invisible ceiling
5. Look at the calendar. Diwali comes just as Delhi's winter begins.
   → calendar strip OCT → NOV with a thermometer dropping
6. On cold, still nights, warmer air sits above cooler air near the ground. … It's called a temperature inversion. And it works like a lid.
   → cross-section: cool blue layer at the ground, warm orange layer above; a glass lid drops over the city; label "TEMPERATURE INVERSION"
7. Last Diwali night, the wind was below 1 metre per second. … The smoke had nowhere to go.
   → wind gauge needle near zero; "WIND < 1 m/s"
8. And under that lid, Delhi's everyday pollution keeps building. Vehicles. Dust. Industry. Garbage burning.
   → icons pile up under the lid one by one
9. In mid-November over the last three years, an analysis of the government's own source model found vehicles made up about half of Delhi's local emissions.
   → pie of LOCAL sources, vehicles slice ≈ 51–53% glows; source tag "CSE ANALYSIS OF IITM DSS · NOV 10–20, 2023–25"
10. And farm fires? … Last year, they dropped sharply. Yet Delhi's post-Diwali air was still the worst in five years.
   → fire-count bars 2024 vs 2025 (Punjab + Haryana, Oct 1–12: 779 → 175); then the PM2.5 bar for 2025 tallest of 2021–2025
11. So there's no single villain. Crackers add the spike. Winter weather traps it. And everyday sources keep it there.
   → three stacked cards: SPIKE · TRAP · STAY
12. That's the end of Diwali week. … Happy Diwali, and tell us which "why" we should answer next.
   → series slide with all six covers lit + Follow/Subscribe; "COMMENT YOUR WHY"
L. So when the haze rolls in this year…
   → the haze drifts back over the skyline → loops into line 1

## Fact checks (checked Nov 4, 2026)
- Diwali 2025 (20 Oct): Delhi's 24-hour average PM2.5 after Diwali was 488 µg/m³ vs 156.6 µg/m³ before, the highest post-Diwali level since 2021 (Climate Trends analysis of CPCB data; PTI via ThePrint, 21 Oct 2025). Peaks above 675 µg/m³ late at night (Down To Earth).
- 2021–2025 post-Diwali PM2.5: 454.5, 168, 319.7, 220, 488 → 2025 highest ("worst in five years").
- India's NAAQS: PM2.5 24-hour standard 60 µg/m³ (CPCB, 2009). 488 ÷ 60 ≈ 8.1 → "about eight times".
- Diwali-night wind below 1 m/s, humidity 60–90%, average temperature 27 °C → 20 °C (Climate Trends, via Down To Earth); temperature inversion described qualitatively.
- Vehicles ≈ 51–53% of LOCAL PM2.5 emissions for 10–20 Nov in 2023, 2024 and 2025 (CSE analysis of IITM's Decision Support System; Down To Earth, 22 Dec 2025). Script says "about half of Delhi's local emissions" (not of all pollution).
- Farm fires: Punjab + Haryana 175 incidents 1–12 Oct 2025 vs 779 in 2024, ≈ 77.5% lower (Climate Trends / NASA FIRMS, via Down To Earth). This is an October window; I say "dropped sharply" and show the window on screen.
- Context, not in the voice: this year Delhi's air was already "very poor" before Diwali (CPCB AQI 350 on 3 Nov 2026, GRAP-II; The Week, 4 Nov 2026). Option for the pinned comment.
- No health claims, no "ban" claims; the 2026 cracker rules aren't mentioned.
