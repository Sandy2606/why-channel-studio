## Research for this Short (RS-01)
- **C17, now read in full** (Nov 14; the scheduled follow-up). Roy, Ramana Murty, Srivastava, Khemani, *MAUSAM* 12(3), 1961, pp. 401–412, doi:10.54302/mausam.v12i3.4212.
  - Who and how: the Rain and Cloud Physics Research Unit at the National Physical Laboratory, New Delhi, ran monsoon trials from July to September, starting in 1957. A dilute salt solution (one pound of salt per gallon of water) was sprayed from the ground by two sprayers for about 3 hours around midday. Updrafts could carry droplets up towards cloud height.
  - Design: seeded vs not-seeded days chosen at random from a book of random numbers. The paper notes this couldn't be followed rigidly in 1957.
  - Results, 1957–59: of **18 trial units, 12 showed a positive tendency, 2 negative, 4 inconclusive**. The authors say it is difficult to reach a "fully dependable conclusion" until the trials run long enough. They wanted 3–4 more years, adding centres at Agra and Jaipur.
  - Purpose: rain stimulation. Delhi is described as a suitable location for "rain stimulation" experiments.
  - **Licence:** CC BY-NC 4.0, so the page is **not reproduced** in a monetised video. An original evidence card with a citation instead (→ RS-07, T4).
- **C14** (PTI via The Week, Oct 28, 2025, citing an IITM report):
  - Dec 1971–Mar 1972 at NPL, ground silver-iodide generators.
  - 22 favourable days: 11 seeded, 11 control.
  - "a tendency for increased rainfall on the seeded days", preliminary.
  - The same report calls 1957 the first trial, and the 2025 trial was meant to ease winter air pollution.
- **C1 / C2 / C3**: the 2025 trial, its pollution purpose, and the Oct 28 result (reused film lines 0.7–0.9).
